Research

Research

My research focuses on the development of statistical methodology for scientific applications, with a particular interest in Bayesian methods. My current work spans statistical genetics and ecology.

Open Projects

01

Unravelling the latent dimensionality of the human phenotype

We investigate the global structure of pleiotropy by asking how many independent dimensions are needed to characterize the human phenome. We develop a dimensionality-reduction framework based on factor analysis and out-of-sample prediction, and apply it to large-scale phenotypic and genetic data.

Statistical Genetics Dimensionality Reduction Pleiotropy
02

Wildlife return after fire

We develop a Bayesian mixture model to study how wildlife return patterns change following fire events. The method is applied to long-term camera-trap data from a Miombo savanna ecosystem in western Tanzania.

Bayesian Statistics Mixture Models Ecology

Previous Projects

01

BayVel: Bayesian modelling of RNA velocity

We developed BayVel, a Bayesian hierarchical model for RNA velocity that directly models raw single-cell RNA sequencing counts. The approach addresses identifiability and provides uncertainty quantification without requiring post-processing.

Bayesian Statistics RNA Velocity Single-cell Genomics
02

High-dimensional MANOVA for semicontinuous data

We developed a MANOVA test for high-dimensional semicontinuous data based on penalized likelihood and permutation inference. The method was applied to microRNA expression and ecological data.

High-dimensional Statistics Hypothesis Testing Semicontinuous Data
03

Nanoparticle growth

We investigated the large-scale behaviour of a stochastic model for nanoparticle nucleation and growth under an unusual parameter regime. Using stochastic simulation and an approximation scheme, we studied the emergence of a limiting particle-size distribution.

Stochastic Modelling Simulation Applied Statistics
